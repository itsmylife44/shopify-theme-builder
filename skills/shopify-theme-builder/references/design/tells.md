# The tells

A tell is a choice that shows a page was produced for no one in particular: the statistical middle of what a model has seen. This is a list of patterns, not a ban list. Banning the first tells only moved the center: the 2025 advice to use "distinctive serifs" and grain gave the 2026 tells of cream, serif and terracotta. So a banned font's favourite replacement is the next tell, and any list of "use this instead" becomes one too.

Use it as a check, line by line, on each Direction card and on the composed Theme: when a choice matches a pattern, it needs a reason from the brief that no other choice meets. With a reason, keep it; without one, derive it again from the brand's world. A look the Creator pinned is kept, whatever it matches.

## Color

- **The category palette.** The colors every shop of this kind uses: cream, beige or bone (`#f5f1ea`, `#efeae0`) with brass, clay, gold or terracotta and espresso text for anything "premium", "natural" or "artisanal"; pastels on white for DTC; purple-to-blue gradients for anything "modern".
- **Near-black with one acid accent**, the predictable opposite of the cream palette.
- **Scattered accents.** The accent on single words, icons, borders and badges, with no section owning it. Color that commits owns whole regions.
- **Light or dark by category** (dark for tech, light for beauty) instead of by the shopper's scene.
- **Pure black by reflex**, or a tinted near-black picked for no reason.
- **Gray text on a colored ground**, or low-contrast body text.

## Type

- **The default faces.** Inter, Roboto, Open Sans, Lato, Montserrat, Poppins, and in Shopify's library the Base Theme's `work_sans_n4`.
- **The escape faces**, which became defaults by being recommended as escapes: Space Grotesk, Space Mono, Syne, Geist, Instrument Sans and Serif, Fraunces, Playfair Display, Cormorant, Lora, Crimson, Newsreader, DM Sans and DM Serif, Outfit, Plus Jakarta Sans, IBM Plex, Bricolage Grotesque (handles like `fraunces_n4`, `playfair_display_n4`, `cormorant_n4`, `instrument_serif_n4`, `space_grotesk_n5`).
- **Genre as a reason.** "It's a creative brief, so a serif"; "it's tech, so a mono"; "it's luxury, so tracked caps". The reason has to come from this brand's world, not its category.
- **Timid hierarchy.** Weights 400 against 600, size steps of 1.5×, a display under three times the body. The best themes run 4× to 13×, or go deliberately flat.
- **The accented word.** One word of a headline in italic, bold or the accent color.
- **Eyebrows everywhere.** A tracked all-caps label above every heading, or small labels in monospace as a "technical" costume. At most one section in three carries one, when it says something true.
- **Middle dots and arrows.** `A · B · C` meta strings, `WORD — fragment` labels, `→` after every link.

## Layout

- **The Shopify formula.** A full-bleed lifestyle hero with a centered "New collection" and "Shop now", a 4-up featured collection, a row of "Free shipping / Easy returns / Secure checkout" with a line icon each, a five-star testimonial carousel, a newsletter with "Join the club, get 10% off". Each section is legitimate; getting all of them, in that order, whatever the brand, is the tell. The order follows the brand's story.
- **The centered hero**: a headline, a line of text and one button, centered over an image or a gradient.
- **Three identical cards** with an icon on top, a heading and two lines.
- **Uniform everything**: the same padding, radius and card height on every element, symmetric everywhere.
- **The same layout family repeated**: image-with-text three times in a row, zig-zagging.
- **Numbers that aren't a sequence**: 01 / 02 / 03 on content with no order.
- **The stat row**: a big number, a small label, repeated four times.

## Surfaces

- **Cards for everything**, cards inside cards, one soft shadow on all of them. Cards are for items a shopper acts on one by one.
- **Accent bars** on the side or top of cards.
- **Glass, blur, grain and noise overlays** as decoration.
- **Gradient text.**
- **More than one radius language**, or a radius that changes by component for no reason.

## Imagery

- **Emoji as icons**, bullets or navigation.
- **Stock people and 3D blobs**: a smiling team at a laptop, abstract shapes, AI-drawn illustration. Real photos or Shopify's honest placeholders instead.
- **A product floating on white with a soft shadow**, when the page around it is white too: frame it on a tint.
- **Icons from mixed families** or stroke weights.

## Copy

- **Filler words**: elevate, curated, seamless, unleash, elevated essentials, timeless, crafted with care, "Welcome to <shop>".
- **Vague headlines** that fit any shop: "Discover our collection", "Quality you can trust".
- **Performative labels**: "Field notes", "Quietly trusted by", "On our shelves".
- **Invented proof**: fake reviews, customer counts, press, stock counters or prices. Leave a block out rather than invent it.
- **One intent, many labels**: "Shop now", "Discover" and "Explore the collection" on one page for the same action.
- **Em dashes** everywhere, straight quotes, `...` for an ellipsis.

## Motion

- **The same fade-and-rise on every section**, a lift on every card hover.
- **Or no states at all**: hovers that do nothing, buttons that snap.
- **Bounces and endless loops.**

The Theme's `motion` setting is one character for the whole Theme; pick it for a reason (a slow zoom for patience, quick fades for a shop people buy from in a hurry, none for a catalog).

## Craft

Only the happy path designed (no empty, error or loading states); unstyled browser defaults; prices without tabular figures; headings that wrap into one orphan word. The catalog sections cover most of these; a Custom Section has to as well.
